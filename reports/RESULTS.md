## 3. Final recommendation (Phase 10, [DL-FINAL])

**Winner: `convnextv2_tiny`.**

The selection rule was fixed in advance: prioritise **OOD macro-F1** and
**calibration (ECE)**, then **size/latency**; raw ID accuracy breaks ties only.

OOD was not evaluated (no quarantined OOD set was collected), so the rule is
applied on the remaining criteria — and this must be stated as a limitation,
because the criterion the rule ranked *first* is missing.

On what was measured:

| Criterion (in rule order) | Result |
|---|---|
| OOD macro-F1 | **not available** — see §5 |
| Calibration (ECE scaled) | convnext **0.0177** vs swin 0.0253 — convnext is 30% better calibrated |
| Latency | convnext **103 ± 13 ms** vs swin 115 ± 69 ms. Swin's spread is **60% of its own mean**; convnext's is 12% |
| Size | effectively equal (106.4 vs 105.1 MB) |
| ID accuracy (tie-break only) | swin +0.63pp = 4 images, inside the noise floor |

Calibration is the deciding criterion, and it is the right one for this
product: the bot's entire behaviour — full answer, "อาจเป็น", or "ยังไม่แน่ใจ" —
is driven by a confidence threshold. A model whose confidence means what it
says is worth more than one that is 4 images more accurate. Latency
consistency points the same way: a farmer on a phone feels the p99, not the
mean, and swin's ±69 ms tail is five times wider.

**`efficientnet_b0` is not viable at its own operating point**, despite being
7× smaller and 3× faster. At its chosen τ = 0.82 it can only answer **52.6%**
of photos; it would abstain on roughly half of everything a farmer sends. That
is the deployment-relevant number for the small model, not its 0.8404 accuracy.

**Ensemble? No.** The two large models are statistically tied on accuracy and
make the *same* mistakes (§6), so averaging them would mostly double the
inference cost without fixing the confusions that matter.

## 6. Per-class & confusion analysis (Phase 6)

Confusion matrices: `outputs/<model>/confusion_matrix.png`.

### The same confusions appear in all three architectures

| True → Predicted | swin | convnext | efficientnet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stem_blight → Canker** | **10** (16% of the class) | 8 | 8 |
| Sooty_mold → Stem_blight | 8 | 8 | 3 |
| Mealybug_infestation → Pink_disease | 7 | 4 | 4 |
| Pink_disease → Stem_cracking_gummosis | 4 | 6 | 4 |
| Yellow_leaf → Canker | 2 | 1 | 3 |

**This is the most important result in this document.** A windowed
transformer, a modern CNN and a mobile CNN have different inductive biases and
were trained independently, yet they fail on the *same pairs*. A confusion
that survives a change of architecture is not a capacity problem — it is a
property of the data and the labels.

Cross-referencing the knowledge base research ([DL-ACTIONS], and the sourcing
notes in `app/data/diseases.json`):

- **Stem_blight → Canker** is the largest confusion in every model, and
  `Stem_blight` is the one class whose causal agent could not be resolved:
  the source dataset attributes it to *Phytophthora palmivora* — which is
  exactly what `Canker` is. The model confusing the two is consistent with
  them being the same organism under two folder names.
- **Yellow_leaf → Canker** matches the finding that yellow leaf is a
  *downstream symptom* of root/stem rot, not an independent disease.
- **Mealybug → Sooty_mold / Pink_disease**: sooty mould grows on mealybug
  honeydew, so the two co-occur on the same plant by definition.

### Weakest class: `Stem_blight`, in every model

| Model | Stem_blight AP | Rank among its classes |
|---|---|---|
| swin_tiny | 0.7672 | worst |
| convnextv2_tiny | 0.7707 | worst |
| efficientnet_b0 | 0.8123 | worst |

The class the research flagged as definitionally unresolved is also the class
every model scores lowest on. That is a quantitative corroboration of a
qualitative finding, and it is actionable: **relabelling `Stem_blight` is
likely worth more than any further model tuning.**

### Lowest recall (swin_tiny)

`Stem_blight` 0.813 · `Pink_disease` 0.842 · `Mealybug_infestation` 0.846 —
the same cluster as above. `Healthy` and `Anthracnose` reach 1.000, but
`Healthy` has only **12 test images**, so its perfect score carries little
weight.

## 8. Limitations (honest)

### 🔴 The reject class was never evaluated

`not_durian` has **zero test images in all three runs** (`support = 0`,
precision/recall/F1 all 0.0, and the class is absent from `auc_per_class`).
`Healthy`, which is auto-split from a flat folder the same way, received 12
test images — so a class receiving exactly zero means it had essentially no
images at all, i.e. **the model was almost certainly never trained on it
either**.

Three consequences, all of which must be disclosed:

1. **Every macro metric in this document is an 11-class average.** Counting
   `not_durian` as 0.0 drops swin's macro-F1 from 0.9330 to **0.8552**.
2. **The open-set claim is unsupported.** The 12-class design ([DL-CLASSES])
   argued that an explicit reject class is safer than thresholding alone.
   This run provides no evidence for that claim either way.
3. **The deployed bot cannot refuse a non-durian photo by class.** The
   `NOT_DURIAN` tier in `app/diagnosis.py` can never fire, so a photo of
   anything at all is forced into one of the 11 durian categories. Only the
   confidence threshold stands between a farmer and a confident wrong answer
   on an irrelevant image.

Fixing it means running `scripts/prepare_negatives.py` to build the negative
set and retraining. Until then, the honest framing is: *this is an 11-class
closed-set classifier with a confidence threshold*, not a 12-class open-set one.

### Other limitations

- **`Healthy` is severely under-represented in test** (12 images vs ~60 for
  every disease). Its 1.000 F1 across models is on a sample too small to
  support the number.
- **OOD was not evaluated at all.** No quarantined out-of-distribution set was
  collected, so the criterion [DL-FINAL] ranked *first* could not be applied,
  and §5 is empty. The model-selection argument therefore rests on calibration
  and latency alone.
- **Interpretability (§4) was not run.** Grad-CAM and the occlusion test are
  what answer this project's central research question, and they are still
  outstanding. The confusion-matrix evidence in §6 is suggestive but indirect.
- **Small, single-orchard dataset** (~5k, one farm in Vinh Long) → limited
  background/cultivar/lighting diversity; high artifact-shortcut risk.
- **Class confusability** is biological (Sooty mold follows Mealybug; multiple
  *Phytophthora* diseases) — some confusion is intrinsic, not just model error.
- **OOD fragility:** web/social images differ in capture, framing, and may be
  mislabelled; expect a real ID→OOD drop.
- **Softmax-threshold OOD** can still be confidently wrong on far-OOD inputs;
  energy/Mahalanobis are stronger but deferred ([DL-THRESH]).
- **Single held-out split** (no k-fold) for timeline reasons → wider CIs.
- **No GPU/data in the generation sandbox** → numbers here must come from your run.
